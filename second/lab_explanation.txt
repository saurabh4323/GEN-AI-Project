================================================================================
LAB 2: GENERATIVE ADVERSARIAL NETWORKS (GAN)
================================================================================

PROJECT OVERVIEW:
This lab implements a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN), a framework where 
two neural networks contest with each other in a game. The goal is to generate 
realistic handwritten digits (MNIST) or fashion items (FashionMNIST) from pure 
random noise.

THE ADVERSARIAL ARCHITECTURE:
1.  The Generator (G):
    A neural network that takes a vector of random noise (latent space) and 
    transforms it into a 28x28 grayscale image. It learns to map noise to the 
    data distribution of the training set.

2.  The Discriminator (D):
    A binary classifier that looks at an image and predicts whether it is "Real" 
    (from the dataset) or "Fake" (produced by the Generator).

3.  The Minimax Game:
    Training involves a continuous loop:
    - The Discriminator is trained to maximize the probability of assigning 
      correct labels (Real vs. Fake).
    - The Generator is trained to maximize the probability of the Discriminator 
      making a mistake (labeling fakes as real).

POST-TRAINING EVALUATION:
Once the GAN is trained, the generated images are passed through a pre-trained 
ResNet18 classifier. This allows us to quantify the "Label Distribution" of our 
generated samples to see if the GAN has captured the diversity of the original 
dataset.

TECHNOLOGIES USED:
- PyTorch (Linear layers, BCELoss)
- Adam Optimizer (standard for GAN stability)
- MNIST / FashionMNIST datasets
- Matplotlib (for sampling generated images)
- ResNet18 (as an external evaluator)
