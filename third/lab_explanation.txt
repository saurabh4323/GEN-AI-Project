================================================================================
LAB 3: VARIATIONAL AUTOENCODERS (VAE)
================================================================================

PROJECT OVERVIEW:
This lab demonstrates the implementation of a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) on 
the MNIST dataset. While standard autoencoders are used for compression, VAEs 
are powerful generative models that learn a probabilistic representation of 
the data.

CORE COMPONENTS:
1.  Encoder:
    Instead of mapping an input image to a single fixed point in a latent space, 
     the VAE encoder produces parameters of a distribution: a Mean (mu) and 
    a Log-Variance (logvar).

2.  Reparameterization Trick:
    To allow gradient descent through random sampling, we sample epsilon from 
    a standard normal distribution and transform it: z = mu + exp(0.5 * logvar) * epsilon.

3.  Decoder:
    The decoder takes the sampled latent vector 'z' and attempts to reconstruct 
    the original 28x28 image.

LOSS FUNCTIONS:
- Reconstruction Loss: Measures how well the decoder recreated the input image 
  (using Binary Cross Entropy).
- KL-Divergence: A regularization term that pushes the learned distribution 
  to be close to a standard normal distribution (Gaussian), ensuring the 
  latent space is continuous and easy to sample from.

VISUALIZATION:
The project includes a comparison between the "Original" digits and their 
"Reconstructed" versions, as well as a grid of entirely "Generated" digits 
sampled from the latent space.

TECHNOLOGIES USED:
- PyTorch (Linear layers, ReLU, Sigmoid)
- MNIST Dataset
- Matplotlib (for visualization of latent reconstructions)
